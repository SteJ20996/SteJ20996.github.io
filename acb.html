The Absent Cognitive Baseline: Theorizing a Structural Gap in AI‑Native College Students' Academic Self‑Assessment

Yizhen Jia & Jiarui Xu · College of Education, Westcliff University

01 · Abstract

Students entering college in the 2026–2027 academic year are among the first cohorts to complete most of their secondary schooling under pervasive generative AI availability. Generative AI can substitute core cognitive operations involved in academic work, including reasoning, synthesis, evaluation, and articulation. This raises questions about what unassisted cognitive experience these learners accumulate, and what internal reference points they draw on when assessing their own academic competence.

This paper proposes the Absent Cognitive Baseline (ACB) as a conceptual framework to name this condition. Drawing on metacognitive theory, self-regulated learning research, epistemic development scholarship, and post-2022 empirical research, the paper argues that academic self-assessment depends on experiential reference points formed during adolescence, that these points are often unevenly developed even under traditional conditions, and that substitutive AI use during the formative high-school window may reduce the independent cognitive encounters on which they depend.

A conceptual model identifies three groups of moderating variables shaping whether ACB emerges. The central argument is that generative AI may change not only what students can do academically, but the conditions under which they come to know what they can do. Interpretative phenomenological analysis is proposed as a methodologically coherent path into the subjective layer the framework theorizes.

02 · The Three Dimensions

What the gap looks like

  • IUnknowabilityAn inability to locate one's own cognitive boundary: no record of unassisted encounters to consult.
  • IIFalse calibrationA miscalibrated sense of understanding derived from AI-generated fluency.
  • IIIDe-normalization of struggleAn erosion of cognitive struggle as a recognizable, expected part of learning.

03 · Model & Method

Moderators, then evidence

Whether ACB emerges is shaped by three groups of moderating variables: use-level (how AI was used), learner-level (who is using it), and environment-level (the settings that reward or resist substitution). Interpretative phenomenological analysis is proposed as the empirical path into the framework's subjective layer.
