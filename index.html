01 — About

Building Reasoning, Not Just Answers

I'm a math educator and doctoral student focused on how students actually learn, not just what they can produce on a test. My teaching spans high school Statistics and Calculus to K–8 foundational math, and my approach centers on breaking complex ideas into intuitive, buildable steps.

At Westcliff University, I'm pursuing a Doctor of Education in Leadership, Curriculum, and Instruction. My research explores graphicacy, data visualization, and the cognitive impact of generative AI on learning, which are questions that sit at the intersection of math education, technology, and how the mind constructs understanding.

Before teaching, I earned both a B.S. and M.S. in Mathematics from Stony Brook University, where I developed deep fluency in theoretical and applied mathematical reasoning, it's a foundation I now bring to every lesson and research question.

Working Paper · 2026

Metacognitively Discordant Completion and the Aware Pass-Through of Non-Understanding in Generative AI Learning

Generative AI lets a learner submit correct, complete work while knowing that understanding has not arrived. This paper names that state — MDC: effort invested, a formed first-person awareness that understanding never occurred, and release of the completion anyway — and argues that no existing construct holds the three together. Illusion, integrity, and disengagement framings each miss the case; the technology's role is amplification, not invention. The paper grounds a planned interpretative phenomenological study anchored in graphicacy.
