Research Prototype · Working name: Delta

Learning that closes the gap.

Not an AI that answers your questions — an AI that figures out how you actually understand, from what you do, not what you say.

Most tutors react to what you ask. But the hardest part of learning is that you often can't tell what you don't understand. Delta is built to find that blind spot — and it asks real effort of you, because effort is exactly where the signal lives.

01 — The Problem

You are a poor judge of your own understanding.

This isn't a criticism — it's a well-documented feature of how minds work. And it's the single biggest thing today's AI tutors miss.

The self-report trap

Current AI tools are reactive: they answer the question you thought to ask. But learners are systematically miscalibrated — we routinely feel we've understood something we haven't. Psychologists call this metacognitive miscalibration, and it's why "just tell the AI what you need" quietly fails. You can't request help with a gap you can't see.

A student can describe their goals. What they usually can't do is name the specific idea that's silently broken, or notice that a method feels like it's working while producing almost no durable learning.

Where Delta lives

Delta closes the loop by watching behavior instead of trusting self-report. It infers your understanding from how you actually answer, what you're confident about, what you get wrong, and how fast that knowledge decays.

The goal isn't to answer your question. It's to find the question you didn't know to ask.

02 — The Counterintuitive Core

Effort isn't the price. It's the mechanism.

Delta deliberately asks more of you than a chatbot does. That's not a rough edge to apologize for — it's the whole point, and there's four decades of research behind it.

Desirable difficulties

Robert Bjork's research shows something unsettling: the study methods that feel effortful and slow — retrieval practice, spacing, interleaving — produce the most durable learning. The ones that feel smooth and easy — rereading, highlighting, watching a clear explanation — produce almost none.

This is precisely why students can't self-select well. Left alone, they optimize for the feeling of fluency, not for real retention. Delta optimizes for the thing that lasts, even when it feels harder in the moment.

Effort is the signal

Every effortful act — attempting before being told, committing to an answer, predicting your own confidence, explaining your reasoning — does double duty. It builds stronger memory, and it emits the behavioral data Delta needs to model you accurately.

A passive tool learns nothing about you. A tool that makes you commit learns everything it needs to adapt.

03 — The Honest Part

What "adaptive" does — and doesn't — mean.

Plenty of edtech promises to match your "learning style." We're deliberately not doing that, because the evidence says it doesn't work.

⚠ Where most "adaptive" claims break

The popular idea of fixed learning styles — that you're a "visual," "auditory," or "kinesthetic" learner and should be taught to match — has been repeatedly tested and found not to hold up. Matching instruction to a self-reported style doesn't improve outcomes. Building a product on it would be building on sand.

So Delta doesn't try to guess what kind of person you are. It adapts to something real and measurable instead: your understanding state, right now, on this specific idea — inferred from evidence, updated every step. That's the version of "adaptive" that has actual science under it.

Six short-answer problems, each a different type.     05 — How It Works

    A four-step loop that never stops adapting.

    Each pass through the loop sharpens the model of you — and the loop itself is the intervention.

    STEP 01

    Effortful diagnosis

    You attempt, commit, and predict your own confidence — before any hint. This surfaces the calibration gap you can't see on your own.

    STEP 02

    Build the model

    Delta updates a live learner model from your behavior: which ideas are shaky, which misconceptions you hold, how fast knowledge is decaying.

    STEP 03

    Adapt the next step

    It chooses your next move — harder variant, a scaffold, a different representation, or a spaced re-test — to keep you in productive struggle.

    STEP 04

    Hold up the mirror

    It shows you what it learned about you. Making the model visible is itself the metacognition training — you learn to see your own gaps.

    06 — What It Adapts On

    Four real dimensions — each with evidence behind it.

    Instead of guessing a personality type, Delta adapts along axes that are measurable and known to matter.

    ↑↓

    Difficulty

    Basis · Zone of proximal development

    Keep every item just past what you can already do alone — hard enough to require real thinking, not so hard you stall. Too easy teaches nothing; too hard teaches nothing.

    ▿

    Misconceptions

    Basis · Diagnostic assessment

    Wrong answers aren't just wrong — they encode which mental model produced them. Delta targets the specific broken idea, not the surface mistake.

    ∞

    Spacing

    Basis · Spaced retrieval & forgetting curves

    It re-surfaces each idea right as you're about to forget it, converting fragile short-term recall into durable long-term memory.

    ∑

    Representation

    Basis · Multiple external representations

    When one framing doesn't land, it tries another — symbolic, visual, numeric, worked example — and remembers which one unlocked this idea for you.

    07 — The Contract

    An honest deal with the learner.

    Delta is demanding by design. Here's exactly what it asks — and exactly what it gives back.

    What it asks of you

    • Attempt before you're told the answer — every time.
    • Commit to a response instead of staying vague.
    • Predict your own confidence, honestly.
    • Explain your reasoning, not just your answer.
    • Come back for the spaced re-tests, even when it feels done.

    What it gives back

    • A map of what you actually know — not what you feel you know.
    • Time spent only on the ideas that are truly shaky.
    • Learning that survives past the test, not just to it.
    • Growing metacognition: you learn to see your own gaps.
    • A tutor that adapts to you instead of the average student.
